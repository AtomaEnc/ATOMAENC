Thorium Fission Cycle

Th-232 → U-233 Breeding — The Physics Behind Real Value

1
Th-232 Absorbs Neutron

Thorium-232 (stable) absorbs a slow neutron and becomes Thorium-233 (unstable) — the first step in the breeding chain.

2
Beta Decay → Pa-233

Th-233 undergoes beta decay (half-life: 22 min) → Protactinium-233. An electron and antineutrino are released.

3
Pa-233 → U-233 (27 days)

Pa-233 undergoes a second beta decay → Uranium-233. This is the primary fissile fuel — highly efficient and powerful.

4
U-233 Chain Reaction ⚡

U-233 fissions with slow neutrons → releases ~200 MeV of energy + 2.49 new neutrons → self-sustaining chain reaction = CRITICALITY.

Frequently Asked

What is Atoma Enc?
Atoma Enc is the world's first energy-backed finite digital standard. With a hard cap of 21,000,000 units, it mimics the scarcity of physical gold while providing real utility through the Sangam Protocol. Every token is backed by verifiable energy output from Thorium micro-reactors — you cannot fake physics.
How is it different from Bitcoin?
Bitcoin introduced digital scarcity through computational Proof-of-Work — miners burn electricity to secure blocks, but that electricity is wasted. Atoma Enc uses Proof-of-Energy (PoE): validators must prove active energy contribution from Thorium micro-reactors. The energy is GENERATED, not destroyed. Result: 0% native fees, 84K TPS, and real-world value backing every token.
What is the Sangam Protocol?
Sangam is the sub-unit of Atoma. 1 Atoma = 100,000 Sangam. This enables perfect micro-divisibility for everyday transactions at any scale — from micro-payments at any merchant to cross-border settlements. The Sangam layer operates as a Layer 2 payment channel with native bridges for instant local currency ↔ Sangam swaps.

What is Proof-of-Energy (PoE) consensus?
PoE is Atoma's consensus mechanism. Each validator node is physically connected to a Thorium micro-reactor via IoT energy meters. To validate a block, the node must submit a cryptographic energy-hash — a tamper-proof measurement of actual megawatts produced. Nodes that go offline lose validation rights. This ties the blockchain's security directly to real-world energy production.
